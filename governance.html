People, stakeholders, and organization

Authority, care, and the politics of where the hubs go

A schedule can be coherent and still fail because no one with land will sign, because the team is exhausted, or because residents do not believe the allocation rule. Organization is part of the design.

Four frames of the organization

Bolman and Deal (2021) argue that leaders misread organizations when they use only one lens. A structural fix will not repair a symbolic insult, and a stirring story will not repair a missing contract. FoodShield needs all four frames, applied to this valley rather than recited as definitions.

Structural frame

The structural frame asks how roles, rules, and coordination are designed. FoodShield uses a program director, a project management office, and work-package leads tied to the work breakdown structure. Authority for contingency and for management reserve is split on purpose, as stated in the charter. The quality lead reports administratively to the program director and may take an unresolved non-conformance to the scenario sponsor without permission. Stage gates at the milestones are the structural way the date stays real. What the structural frame cannot do by itself is make Abbotsford, the regional district, and Fraser Health into one employer. They are separate legal bodies. Coordination is by agreement, not by pretending there is a single org chart over them.

Human resource frame

The human resource frame asks whether the organization fits the people in it. Pandemic logistics depends on drivers, warehouse leads, public-health liaisons, and clerks who will be asked to work while their own families are ill. The staffing plan therefore pays a surge roster and refuses a plan that only works if people volunteer their nights. Training starts in 2028 so the team is not asked to form, storm, and run a full-scale exercise in the same season (Tuckman, 1965). Hygiene factors in Herzberg’s sense — safe schedules, working equipment, intelligible supervision — are funded because motivators do not compensate for a broken dock or a late paycheque (Herzberg, 1968).

Political frame

The political frame asks who has power, who wants the hub, and who can stall it. Five municipalities will not be equally excited by five different building sizes. Abbotsford’s prototype is the largest line item because it is the specification, not because the city has won a contest. That sentence will not survive first contact with local politics unless the champion, in the scenario, is willing to say it in public. Indigenous governments are not a political interest group inside the project. They hold authority. The Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act (2019) is the reason the political frame stops at the edge of consent. Inside the project team, the political risk is quieter: schedule pressure will try to capture the quality lead. The independent reporting path is the counter-move.

Symbolic frame

The symbolic frame asks what the work means. The name FoodShield, the memory of empty shelves, and the memory of the 2021 floods (Gillett et al., 2022) will be used in communication because people remember stories more easily than earned-value indices. Symbols become a defect when a groundbreaking is held on land whose government has not agreed, or when a full-scale exercise is staged for cameras before the offline test is real. Debriefs, published allocation rules, and a handover that includes an operating budget are the rituals that matter. A logo is optional.

Three spheres of systems management

Schwalbe’s three-sphere model says an information-and-operations project has to satisfy the business, the organization, and the technology at the same time (Schwalbe, 2019). Success in one sphere with failure in another is still failure. A platform that allocates perfectly, used by a municipality that does not trust the rule, does not feed anyone.

Business

The business sphere is the public result: availability, access, utilization, and stability of food for priority populations (FAO, 2008), inside a fixed ceiling and a fixed date. The payback cases belong here, including the uncomfortable household-only case. So does the decision not to become a permanent grocery system. Value is the avoided break in food access during a wave, plus a receiver who can afford to keep the option alive after 2029.

Organization

The organization sphere is staffing, agreements, rightsholder consent, union and driver conditions, and the habits of municipal emergency programs that already exist under the Emergency and Disaster Management Act (2023). FoodShield does not replace local emergency plans. It gives them a food-logistics tool and a trigger protocol. If those plans and this program disagree about who declares activation, the disagreement is an organizational defect and is tested in the tabletop, not discovered in a wave.

Technology

The technology sphere is refrigeration, backup power, the fleet powertrain, telematics, and the logistics platform. Technology choices are accepted only when the organization can operate them on a bad day: winter range, offline mode, alarms that do not depend on the platform, and data the next owner can export. A sophisticated stack that requires the vendor’s night shift to move a pallet has failed this sphere even if the demonstration looked modern.

The three spheres cut across the four frames. Technology without the structural contracts, the human skill, the political consent, and a believable public story will sit unused. The spheres are the integration test; the frames are the diagnostic when a sphere is stuck.

How the team is structured

Scenario authority and the delivery team

  • Scenario sponsor and champion: fictional FoodShield Task Force, champion named in the brief as Mark Carney. Holds management reserve. Does not hold municipal or Indigenous jurisdiction.
  • Program director: single point of accountability for the baseline.
  • Project management office: schedule, cost, risk, change secretariat, reporting.
  • Infrastructure lead: hubs, cold chain, utilities.
  • Fleet and logistics lead: duty cycle, depots, delivery model.
  • Food-safety and reserves lead: specifications, suppliers, rotation, recall.
  • Digital product owner: spirals, backlog, offline acceptance. Not the only person who can stop a bad release; quality can stop it too.
  • Partnership lead: municipalities, community agencies, and the process with Indigenous governments.
  • Quality and evaluation lead: audits, test gates, UFV evaluation interface, direct path to the sponsor for unresolved non-conformances.

Receiving operations after December 31, 2029 are not a branch of this tree. They belong to the authorities that sign the handover. The tree is dismantled on purpose at close, except where a named person transfers under a municipal or health-authority employment offer.

Rightsholders

Putting Indigenous governments in a power-interest grid beside a cloud vendor would repeat a category error. They are rightsholders. The project does not “manage” them. Where a hub, a road approach, a stock, or a research activity touches their lands or their food system, work proceeds only with their decision. This proposal does not freeze a list of nations. An incomplete list with a confident tone would be worse than a process. The partnership lead’s first planning deliverable is a correctly made record of the governments and Indigenous organizations that choose to enter discussions, produced with those governments rather than about them. Stó:lō peoples of the river are the starting point for that work in this valley, not a complete map of it.

Staffing plan

The $24,000,000 core-staff line and the $8,000,000 secondment line cover 2027 through 2029 only. Table 14 is a planning complement, not a list of named incumbents. Hiring in the scenario still has to follow whatever employment law applies to the employing body. This proposal does not invent a shadow civil service.

The peak complement is intentionally near fifty full-time equivalents, matching the order-of-magnitude note on the cost page. It is not fifty people on day one. Initiating and planning run lean. The surge roster is trained before it is large. Secondments from municipalities are the main way the people who will inherit the system learn it before the keys are offered. A secondment that is only a meeting invite will not survive handover.

Motivation framework

People doing this work are not a generic private-sector team that needs a sales contest. Many will be public servants, health staff, drivers, and community workers. Perry and Wise (1990) describe public service motivation as a commitment to the public interest, duty, and compassion. Recruitment and recognition should talk about neighbours’ access to food. They should not talk about hustle.

Herzberg’s distinction still applies inside that public motive (Herzberg, 1968). The work itself, responsibility for a hub, and fair recognition after a hard exercise are motivators. Pay, safe equipment, predictable time off between waves of overtime, and supervisors who can explain a decision are hygiene factors. If hygiene fails, the public motive curdles into cynicism, and cynicism during an activation is an operational risk. The budget for paid surge is a motivation control as much as it is a finance control.

Tuckman’s sequence is used as a scheduling constraint, not as a team-building game (Tuckman, 1965). The core team needs forming and the inevitable conflict over methods in 2027 and early 2028, while the cost of conflict is still a meeting. The full-scale exercise is too late to discover that fleet and food safety do not share a definition of “ready.”

Three practices follow:

  • Recognition is specific and public only when the person wants it public. Naming a driver in a press release without asking is not recognition.
  • Overtime in exercises and in any real activation during the project is paid, logged, and followed by time off. Heroic unpaid labour is reported as a quality escape, not as a success story.
  • Community volunteers are not the staffing plan for the 24-hour delivery target. Volunteers may supplement a paid roster. They do not replace it. Motivation language that leans on sacrifice from the people with the least slack is a political and ethical failure, and it is also a brittle plan.
