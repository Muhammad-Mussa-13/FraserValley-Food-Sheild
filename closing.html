Closing and handover

Operational ownership by December 31, 2029

Closing is a process group, not a party. The project is finished only when the people who will run the hubs have accepted them, funded them, and practiced them.

What closing is for

The sixth edition of the PMBOK Guide treats closing as the work of confirming that the work is done, completing contracts, and releasing the organization (PMI, 2017). For FoodShield that definition is necessary and not sufficient. A commissioned refrigerator with no operator, no operating budget, and no agreed trigger is an asset on a register and a failure of purpose. Closing therefore transfers operational ownership to regional municipal authorities and to Fraser Health for the health role, on or before December 31, 2029.

Shadow operation runs from July through December 2029, overlapping the last construction punch lists. It does not extend past the deadline. Any retainer after that date is a contract the receivers procure with their own money. The project ceiling is closed.

Exit criteria

Handover instruments are signed only when every item below is true, or when the scenario sponsor has waived it in writing with the residual risk attached. A waiver of a critical food-safety item is not available to the program director alone. That limit is stated in the quality plan.

  • Milestone M6, the full-scale exercise, has a pass from the quality lead, or every critical defect has a closed record.
  • Five primary hubs have occupancy and commissioning records, including temperature mapping and a backup-power test under load.
  • The fleet has met the 24-hour community-point target in the exercise script, including a winter-capable solution for the Hope run.
  • The platform has completed a scripted day with the wide-area network disconnected, and the public allocation text matches the software rule.
  • First-fill stock is reconciled to the lot register. Expired or quarantined product is not in the countable reserve.
  • Each receiver has a named accountable official and an operating budget adopted, or scheduled for adoption, at a dollar level the official is willing to defend. The $20,000,000 annual allowance on the cost page is a placeholder, not that budget.
  • Activation authority is written against the Emergency and Disaster Management Act (2023), not against the fictional champion.
  • Any site on Indigenous land has a handover path agreed by that government. If agreement is absent, that site is not in the acceptance set, and the omission is reported rather than papered over.

Who receives what

Joint ownership of every truck and every pallet is attractive in a workshop and slow in a council calendar. The default below is designed to be signable by June 30, 2029. Partners may replace it with a regional service agreement earlier than that. They may not replace it with an unresolved committee after that date, because the deadline would then be lost to a governance argument the schedule already identified as risk R10.

Every building transfer includes a covenant that the 90-day priority reserve and the published allocation rule survive a change in council mood. Selling the stock to balance a budget, or limiting it to residents of the host city while the charter geography is wider, is a breach of the handover, not a local policy choice. The legal form of that covenant is for the lawyers in the $7,000,000 procurement-and-legal line to draft. The requirement to have it is a project requirement.

Transition steps

The steps overlap. They are numbered in the order a receiver should be able to tell the story, not as a claim that each one waits for the previous one to end. Dates assume the milestone baseline is holding.

  1. Open the closing file in July 2029

    The program director appoints a transition lead who is not also the person trying to finish the last dock. The file contains the asset list, the contract list, the non-conformance list, the privacy inventory, and the names of the officials each receiver has designated. If a receiver has not designated anyone, that absence is escalated in July, not in December.

  2. Reconcile what was built with what the charter promised

    Each work-package acceptance record is matched to the work breakdown structure. Items removed by the descoping ladder are listed as removed, with the date of the change-board decision. Receivers are not asked to accept a verbal story that a van count or a software feature was “essentially” delivered.

  3. Complete the physical condition survey

    An independent inspection, paid from the audit line, records deficiencies, warranties, spare parts, calibrations, and the as-built drawings. Temperature-mapping reports and backup-power tests are attached to the rooms they describe. A deficiency that is merely cosmetic can transfer with a price. A deficiency that breaks food safety cannot.

  4. Count the food

    A wall-to-wall stock count compares the lot register with the pallets. Quarantined, recalled, and short-dated product is separated. The rotation procedure, including the assumed loss factor, is walked with the person who will own the count after December. The functional-exercise recall is repeated if the first one missed its time limit.

  5. Transfer procedures and prove competency

    Site leads, depot leads, and allocation clerks from the receiving bodies work the shadow shifts. Competency is a signed checklist against the tasks in the full-scale script: receive a lot, refuse a lot, dispatch under the rule, run the offline hour, and hand over to the next shift. A secondment that attended meetings and did not work a shift does not count.

  6. Put the operating budget in front of the receivers

    By October 31, 2029 each receiver has a draft operating budget for 2030: energy, staff, insurance, vehicle sinking fund, food rotation, exercises, software licences, and the secretariat share. The project may help write it. The project may not fund it. Acceptance without this draft is a failed close, even if the buildings are beautiful. Councils that need a later vote record the date of that vote and the interim arrangement that keeps the doors staffed.

  7. Close or assign the contracts

    Construction contracts are closed against the deficiency list. Warranties are assigned to the new building owner and the manufacturer confirms the assignment in writing. Vehicle warranties and telematics export rights move with the fleet. The software contract is either assigned, with the vendor’s consent, or replaced by a receiver-led contract that starts January 1, 2030. Hosting prepaid only to December 31, 2029 is not quietly treated as a gift of later years.

  8. Move the data and then remove project access

    Stock data, asset data, and any personal information are mapped to the receiving public bodies. The privacy schedule says who may look up a household delivery and for how long that record lives. On the day of acceptance, project-office accounts lose access. UFV keeps only what the ethics agreement and the data schedule allow. A copied database “just in case” on a staff laptop is a privacy incident, not a backup.

  9. Sign the activation protocol in the names of the real authorities

    The protocol states who can activate, what indicators short of a formal declaration are enough, how Hope is served if Highway 1 is cut, how Fraser Health’s health criteria bind the clerks, and how a disagreement is decided inside one operational period. It cites the provincial emergency statute. It does not cite the fictional task force as a command. The 2028 tabletop’s lessons are either in the protocol or listed as rejected, with a reason.

  10. Run the full-scale exercise as a gate, then close the defects

    The exercise remains dated September 30, 2029 so that October, November, and December exist for defect closure and for a second shift of shadow operation. A failure is reported as a failure. If critical defects are still open on December 15, the sponsor chooses a partial acceptance — naming the hubs that are actually ready — or records that the scenario missed its charter. Partial acceptance must still leave a safe reserve and a truthful public note. It must not leave a dark building described as a regional system.

  11. Reconcile the money

    The final cost report ties every line in the budget to commitments, actuals, and remaining reserve. Unspent contingency and management reserve are returned to the scenario sponsor in the fiction, not spent in the last fortnight on unplanned scope. In any real appropriation they would return to the funding authority under that authority’s rules. The independent audit report is an attachment, including any qualification.

  12. Sign acceptance and dissolve the project office

    On or before December 31, 2029 the designated officials sign the acceptance instruments, the covenant on the reserve, and the residual-risk statement. The program director issues the close report. Staff are released, transferred under a real employment offer, or returned to their home municipality. Keys, alarm codes, and vendor contacts are on the asset file the new owner holds. The project mailbox is shut. From January 1, 2030 a resident who needs the system reaches a municipality, the regional district, or Fraser Health, not a project that no longer exists.

What does not transfer

  • The fictional champion’s role. It ends with the scenario. It is not an ongoing emergency power.
  • Operating funds, vehicle replacement after the sinking fund the receivers themselves create, and software licences beyond December 31, 2029, unless a receiver has signed a new contract.
  • Clinical services, animal-disease eradication, diking programs, and income assistance. Coordination seats may continue. Mandates do not move.
  • A promise to feed people outside the charter geography. Mutual aid with Langley or Metro Vancouver can be added later by the receivers. It is not implied by acceptance.
  • Research access for UFV beyond the written data schedule. Academic sponsorship of this proposal is not a perpetual key to operational data.

Records and lessons

Lessons are collected at each quarterly gate, not only in a December workshop when people are leaving. The final workshop in November 2029 asks three questions: which control in the risk register actually existed on the ground, which acceptance test was nearly waived under date pressure, and which community or government was heard too late. Answers go into the close report in attributable form. Anonymous praise is allowed. Anonymous blame of a partner government is not.

The archive holds the charter, baselines, change log, as-built drawings, temperature maps, exercise reports, stock reconciliation, privacy schedule, financial audit, and the acceptance instruments. The secretariat keeps the operational set. A second set is stored as the receivers’ records-management rules require. The project website, if it remains online as a student artefact, keeps the disclaimer and does not start presenting itself as the live operating manual.

The test of the close is ordinary and strict. On the first Monday of 2030, a person on shift at the Chilliwack depot can find a lot, refuse a bad one, and name the official who may activate the system, without calling a project manager. If that is true across the hubs that were accepted, FoodShield has closed. If it is true only in the close report, it has not.
