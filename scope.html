Scope, charter, and deliverables

What the program will finish, and what it will not

The charter fixes purpose, authority, and acceptance. The work breakdown structure names the products. The boundary section is there so a later request to “just add” retail replacement or clinical care cannot be absorbed without a trade.

Project charter

A charter authorizes the project and gives the project manager a baseline against which change can be seen (PMI, 2017). In this classroom scenario the charter would be approved by June 30, 2027. Until that gate, the figures in this website are a proposal baseline, not an approved project baseline.

Table 2. Charter summary
Charter element Statement
Purpose Prepare the defined Fraser Valley service area to maintain availability, access, utilization, and stability of food for priority populations during a pandemic or a compound pandemic-and-flood emergency (FAO, 2008).
High-level product Five resilience hubs, an emergency distribution fleet with depots, a digital logistics platform with an offline mode, a first-fill strategic reserve, trained people, and signed handover to operating authorities.
Business need Ordinary retail and just-in-time processing are efficient in calm weeks and brittle in a wave that removes labour, closes Highway 1, or both. The 2021 atmospheric-river floods showed how quickly this geography can be isolated (Gillett et al., 2022).
Budget ceiling CAD $1,000,000,000 in scenario funds. The ceiling is fixed. Overruns are met by the descoping ladder on the cost page, not by a larger envelope.
Schedule ceiling Operational ownership transferred, and the project closed, on or before December 31, 2029.
Project manager authority May commit contingency for identified risks up to the contingency reserve, and may not spend the management reserve. May not add scope. Change requests that trade scope, time, or cost go to the change board described under schedule control.
Quality bar No critical food-safety non-conformance open at commissioning. Allocation rules published before the full-scale exercise. Personal information, if collected, assessed under British Columbia’s public-sector privacy statute.

Project champion and the fictional task force

Within this assignment, the project champion is Mark Carney, acting through a fictional Fraser Valley FoodShield Task Force convened for the scenario with the University of the Fraser Valley and the Chaacha brief. The champion’s job, in project terms, is to protect the purpose, break deadlocks that the project manager cannot break, and hold the management reserve (PMI, 2017). Carney’s published argument that public and private decisions should count values beyond immediate market price is relevant background for a long-lived food-security asset (Carney, 2021). That book is not a funding announcement, and this proposal must not be read as one.

The scenario does not create legal authority. A champion on paper cannot bind Abbotsford, the Fraser Valley Regional District, Fraser Health, or any Indigenous government. Initiation therefore includes a real decision: either the classroom charter stays a teaching document, or any later real-world use would have to be authorized by the bodies that actually hold land, public health powers, and Indigenous jurisdiction. The Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act (2019) is a constraint on that second path. Activities on Indigenous lands proceed only with the governments whose authority is affected, on a government-to-government basis.

Objectives and success criteria

Objectives are written so that a stage-gate review can say yes or no. Dates match the milestone table.

  1. By June 30, 2029, commission five primary hubs whose combined dry and chilled capacity can hold the reserve described below, each with tested backup power for its cold and dry rooms and a passed temperature-mapping study.
  2. By March 31, 2029, place in service a fleet and depot network able to deliver emergency food parcels to designated community points in every municipality named in the charter within 24 hours of a declared activation.
  3. By September 30, 2029, accept a logistics platform that can allocate on-hand stock to those points using the published priority rule, and that has been operated for a full exercise day with the wide-area network disconnected.
  4. By October 31, 2029, complete a full-scale exercise that draws stock, moves fleet, and uses the offline platform, with critical defects closed or formally waived by the change board before handover.
  5. By December 31, 2029, execute the handover: asset registers, operating procedures, privacy schedule, warranties, and a receiver operating budget that is not funded from the project ceiling.

The reserve objective uses a transparent planning assumption, not a hidden tonnage. The working service population is 320,000. Priority support is sized for 30 percent of that population, or 96,000 people, for 90 days of shelf-stable food, plus a 14-day chilled buffer for a smaller medically and culturally indicated subset. Energy planning uses 2,100 kilocalories per person per day as a starting ration, adjusted for age, clinical needs, and preference during planning. Statistics Canada census-subdivision counts replace the 73,273-person remainder allowance before the reserve is tendered (Statistics Canada, 2022). The project does not promise to feed every resident, for an unlimited period, as a parallel grocery system.

Work breakdown structure

The work breakdown structure is product-oriented. Activities are used to build and accept deliverables; they are not themselves the deliverables (PMI, 2017; Larson & Gray, 2021). Work package owners are assigned in planning, when posts are filled. Until then, ownership sits with the program director.

Table 3. Level-2 work breakdown structure
Code Deliverable Acceptance evidence
1.0 Fraser Valley FoodShield program Closed project, accepted by the scenario sponsor and by the receiving authorities named in the handover agreements.
1.1 Governance and initiation Signed classroom charter, rightsholder engagement record, locked census extract, and a stakeholder register.
1.2 Resilience hub network Five hubs commissioned: Abbotsford, Chilliwack, Mission, Hope, and Kent–Agassiz. Occupancy, food-safety, backup-power, and accessibility checks complete.
1.3 Cold chain and utilities resilience Refrigeration on an N+1 basis for reserve rooms, backup generation and battery, potable-water storage, and flood and seismic measures accepted against the design flood and seismic criteria set at the design gate.
1.4 Distribution fleet and depots Heavy refrigerated units, medium trucks, last-mile vans, and two depots in service. Duty-cycle test passed for the Hope run, including a winter trial.
1.5 Digital logistics platform Inventory, allocation, recall trace, and offline mode accepted. Security test and privacy impact assessment closed. Paper and radio procedure accepted as a fallback.
1.6 Strategic reserves First fill of shelf-stable, chilled, therapeutic, and culturally indicated stocks, with lot traceability and a rotation standard operating procedure.
1.7 Workforce, training, and exercises Competency sign-off for hub, fleet, and allocation roles. Seminar, tabletop, functional, and full-scale exercises completed.
1.8 Community partnership and equity Published allocation rule, accessibility and language supports, and partnership agreements that name decision rights. Indigenous food-sovereignty initiatives only where the relevant government has asked for them.
1.9 Monitoring, quality, and closing Monthly control reports, independent audit, lessons-learned report, financial reconciliation, and the handover package.

Satellite spaces in Harrison Hot Springs and in electoral areas are distribution points served from the five hubs. They are not a sixth primary hub. If estimates rise, satellite fit-out is cut before any primary hub, and before food-safety systems.

Scope boundaries

Table 4. Inclusions and exclusions
Inclusions Exclusions
Design, construction or major retrofit, and commissioning of the five hubs, including cold rooms and dry storage. Routine grocery retail, a permanent public supermarket, or ongoing food subsidy after handover.
Backup power, water, sanitation, and flood and seismic hardening required to keep reserve rooms operable. Regional flood defences, diking policy, or rebuilding private farms after a flood.
Fleet, depots, charging, telematics, and the training needed to meet the 24-hour delivery target. Public transit, ambulance fleets, and general municipal vehicle replacement.
A logistics platform, cybersecurity for that platform, field devices, and an offline procedure. A regional electronic health record, hospital information systems, or consumer grocery e-commerce.
First-fill reserves and the rotation method through December 31, 2029, including infant formula, medical nutrition, allergen-controlled foods, and culturally indicated foods specified with partner governments and agencies. Income assistance, disability benefits, or replacing the mandate of existing food banks. Food banks are partners at distribution points, not assets to be absorbed.
Exercises, competency sign-off, and the handover package, including a receiver operating-budget template. Operating costs after December 31, 2029. Receivers fund operations. The project ceiling does not endow a permanent staff.
Coordination protocols with the Canadian Food Inspection Agency when an animal-health emergency, such as highly pathogenic avian influenza, coincides with a human pandemic. Leading an animal-disease eradication campaign, compensating producers for culled flocks, or regulating farms.
Accessibility of public-facing hubs and information, consistent with the direction of the Accessible British Columbia Act (2021). Clinical care, vaccination clinics, and hospital surge capacity, except for a coordination seat with Fraser Health.
Service to the municipalities and electoral areas named in the charter. Construction outside that geography. Langley and Metro Vancouver are mutual-aid neighbours, not build sites.

SWOT analysis

The SWOT below is a strategic reading of this program in this place. It is an input to risk responses and to scope protection. It is not a list of slogans. Strengths and weaknesses are internal to the program as designed. Opportunities and threats are external (Larson & Gray, 2021).

Strengths

The ceiling is large enough to build more than a warehouse of canned goods. Pairing shelf-stable stock with chilled capacity, fleet, and an offline information system matches the way food actually fails: sometimes the food is there and the trucks or the trust are not, and sometimes the power is the thing that fails. The valley already produces food at provincial scale, so a reserve strategy can use regional suppliers where safety and reliability allow, instead of relying only on distant emergency rations. A fixed deadline forces handover to be designed in 2027 rather than remembered in December 2029. Using both waterfall and spiral lifecycles puts the control method on the kind of work: buildings reward a stable design, and software rewards staged discovery (Boehm, 1988).

Weaknesses

Eight percent contingency on a program with retrofit, floodplain, and food-commodity uncertainty is at the low end of what a capital program usually carries. The compensation is a descoping ladder, and that ladder will disappoint someone. The working population still contains a 73,273-person allowance that must be replaced before tender. Electric heavy vehicles may not meet the Hope duty cycle in winter, so the fleet strategy can be forced into a mixed powertrain late in procurement. The project team does not yet exist, and a team formed in 2027 must be past its early conflict before it can run a 2029 full-scale exercise (Tuckman, 1965). Finally, a fictional champion cannot unlock land.

Opportunities

The same hubs that hold pandemic stock can support a short, declared response to a flood or a major outage, if activation rules are written that way and if scope creep into everyday food banking is refused. UFV can supply evaluation capacity so the program learns during spirals rather than only at the final report. Indigenous food-sovereignty work, where a government wants it, can make the reserve more legitimate and more nutritionally appropriate than a generic ration. A published allocation rule is also a public good: it gives councils and residents something to argue with before a crisis, which is a better time to argue.

Threats

A pandemic, or a major animal-health emergency in the poultry sector, could arrive during construction and remove both contractors and operators. Highway and dike failures can repeat (Gillett et al., 2022). Commodity prices and construction inflation can consume the fixed ceiling. Municipalities may compete for hub siting, and a fight about prestige can delay the Hope hub, which is the node that matters most if the valley is split. Public trust can fail if priority rules are seen as favouring the largest cities. Cyber attack on a highly visible emergency system is attractive to an adversary precisely when the system is needed. These threats are carried into the risk register with owners and controls, not left as a SWOT paragraph.

Assumptions and constraints

Assumptions

  • Scenario funds are available on January 1, 2027 and are not waiting on a real appropriation cycle. If this proposal were ever reused outside the classroom, that assumption would be false until a legislature or a council made it true.
  • Sites or long leases can be secured in 2027. If a site fails, the schedule slips or the descoping ladder is used. The project does not quietly move a hub onto land it does not have authority to use.
  • Receiving municipalities, the regional district, and Fraser Health are the intended operators in the scenario. None of them has been asked to endorse this document.
  • A share of priority food can be sourced through competitive supply arrangements that meet federal food-safety rules (Safe Food for Canadians Regulations, 2018).
  • Personal information for delivery to people who cannot reach a community point can be minimized. Where it cannot, the privacy assessment will constrain the design.

Constraints

  • The budget ceiling and the December 31, 2029 date are both hard.
  • Food placed in the reserve must meet Canadian food-safety requirements, including preventive controls and traceability where the regulations apply.
  • Data that are personal information in the custody of a public body stay inside the rules of the Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (1996). Vendor systems are not given a free hand to store that information outside Canada.
  • The program coordinates with provincial emergency management under the Emergency and Disaster Management Act (2023). It does not invent a parallel command structure that ignores the province or local authorities.
  • All public materials, including this website, carry the academic disclaimer. Hypothetical partnerships are not to be described as agreed.
