Time and schedule

Three years, two lifecycles, one deadline

The baseline runs from January 1, 2027 to December 31, 2029. Buildings follow a waterfall. The logistics platform follows a spiral. Both must be finished in time for one full-scale exercise and a real handover.

Scheduling approach

This page is a Level-2 planning schedule. It is accurate enough to show phase logic, gates, and the deadline. It is not a critical-path method network with durations in days, because those durations would be invented before sites, soils, and vendors are known. During planning, each work package in the work breakdown structure will receive an activity list, duration estimates, logic ties, and a resource load (PMI, 2017; Larson & Gray, 2021). The quarter bars below are the envelope those detailed schedules must fit.

Calendars respect construction seasons in a wet valley, but they do not assume that winter is lost. Interior fit-out, equipment setting, software spirals, reserve contracting, and training continue through the wet months. What the schedule refuses to do is commission cold rooms, fill them, and exercise them in the same month. Temperature mapping, staff competency, and a stock reconciliation each need their own window.

Lifecycle model

Using one lifecycle for the whole program would either freeze the software too early or keep redesigning the buildings too late. The program therefore runs two predictive models on purpose, and integrates them at named gates.

Waterfall for physical infrastructure and fleet hardware

Hubs, refrigeration, depots, and vehicle specifications follow a sequential model: requirements, design, build, commission, hand over. A cold room sized incorrectly is expensive to reopen, and a flood elevation chosen after the slab is poured is not a design choice. The first hub, Abbotsford, is the prototype. Its requirements and design are approved before construction starts. Later hubs repeat that specification with site-specific civil works, which is faster than inventing five architectures. Fleet hardware is the same kind of problem. The duty cycle, including the grade and the winter temperature east of Chilliwack, is frozen before units are ordered. Change after the order is a formal change, because a mixed fleet that was not planned is a maintenance burden for the municipalities that inherit it.

Spiral, with agile delivery inside it, for the digital platform

Boehm’s spiral model is organized around risk, not around a single requirements freeze (Boehm, 1988). Each cycle sets objectives, analyses risk, builds a prototype or increment, and evaluates it before the next commitment. That fits a logistics platform whose hardest risks are adoption, offline behaviour, privacy, and interface with municipal staff who do not yet exist as a team. Inside each spiral, the product owner runs short iterations, a backlog, and working software reviews, which is the agile practice appropriate to a defined increment (Project Management Institute & Agile Alliance, 2017).

Five spirals are planned:

  1. Discovery (second half of 2027). Allocation rules, offline scenarios, and privacy constraints. The prototype may be a clickable process, not a production system.
  2. Inventory truth (2028). Lot, location, temperature-alarm status, and recall. Alarms also exist on a path that does not depend on this software.
  3. Allocation (late 2028 to early 2029). The published priority rule applied to on-hand stock and to community points.
  4. Fleet and field (first half of 2029). Dispatch, proof of delivery, and devices that degrade gracefully.
  5. Hardening (second and third quarters of 2029). Security test, disconnect test, and operational acceptance. No new functional scope in this spiral except defect repair.

The integration rule is simple. The full-scale exercise in the third quarter of 2029 uses both the commissioned physical network and the hardened platform. A spiral that misses that exercise does not get a quiet extension past December 31. Features that are not required for inventory truth, allocation, and offline operation are the first items on the descoping ladder.

Five process groups

The sixth edition of the PMBOK Guide describes five process groups. They are not five phases that occur once. Monitoring and controlling runs beside the other groups for the life of the project (PMI, 2017). The seventh edition later reorganized the standard around principles and performance domains (PMI, 2021). This proposal keeps the five groups because they make the control story legible, and it uses the later edition as a warning against treating the groups as bureaucracy. Stewardship and uncertainty still apply: a green dashboard that hides a site the team does not control is not control.

Critical path and float

The driving path into December 31, 2029 is: charter and sites, Abbotsford design, Abbotsford construction, commissioning of the five-hub network, first-fill of reserves into accepted rooms, full-scale exercise, defect closeout, and handover. Design slippage on the prototype hub moves every downstream date. That is why M3 exists in 2028. A hub that is pretty in June 2029 and stocked in November has missed the exercise, and an unexercised warehouse is not an accepted deliverable.

The platform path is parallel and joins the driving path at M6. Spiral 5 has little float. Spirals 2 through 4 have float only for features that are not on the exercise script. Fleet initial capability in March 2029 has about two quarters of float before the exercise, which is deliberate: drivers need time on the vehicles before a scored scenario. Community-grant administration has the most float and is the wrong place to “catch up” by stealing staff from commissioning.

Hope is not the longest construction path, but it is the path with the least strategic float. If floodwater splits the valley, stock west of the break cannot serve Hope, Harrison, and parts of the eastern corridor. The schedule may float Hope’s interior finishes. It may not float Hope’s weather-tight reserve room past the 2029 first-fill window.

Baseline and change control

After M2, scope, schedule, and cost are one baseline. A change to any one of them is a change request. The project manager may approve substitutions that do not move a milestone, do not spend contingency, and do not alter food-safety or accessibility acceptance criteria. Everything else goes to a change board chaired by the program director, with the quality lead present and with a direct report to the scenario sponsor when a request would consume management reserve or cut protected scope.

Monthly control uses earned value in a form municipal sponsors can read: planned value, earned value, actual cost, schedule variance, cost variance, the schedule performance index, and the cost performance index (PMI, 2017). A index below 1.0 for two consecutive months on the hub path triggers a recovery plan at the next monthly review, not at the next annual report. Quarterly gates can stop a work package. The program date itself is not moved. If the date cannot be met inside the ceiling, the board removes scope from the bottom of the descoping ladder and records the value that was given up.

Time reserves sit inside the bars as management float on non-critical packages. They are not a silent fourth quarter after December 2029. Closing activities start in July 2029 while punch-list work is still underway, because contract novation, privacy schedules, and receiver budget votes take longer than a ribbon cutting.
