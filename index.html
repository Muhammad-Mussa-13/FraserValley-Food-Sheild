Purpose

Food security exists when all people, at all times, have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and preferences (Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations [FAO], 2008). That definition has four dimensions that must hold at once: availability, access, utilization, and stability. A pandemic attacks all four. Illness and border measures reduce farm and plant labour, so availability falls. Illness, isolation, and income loss cut access. Power failures and rushed handling weaken utilization because food that is unsafe is not food. Stability fails when a short shock becomes a season without stock.

Fraser Valley FoodShield is a three-year program, opening on January 1, 2027 and closing on December 31, 2029, that would build the physical, fleet, and information capacity to protect those four dimensions for a defined service area. The program does not replace grocery retail, income support, or clinical care. It creates a public capability that can be activated when ordinary supply cannot reach priority populations, and it hands that capability to local authorities with an operating plan, not only with keys to a building.

The proposal uses the five process groups in the sixth edition of A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (Project Management Institute [PMI], 2017) because the brief asks for initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, and closing as a visible structure. It also uses the seventh edition’s emphasis on stewardship, uncertainty, and delivery of value (PMI, 2021), so that a process checklist does not become a substitute for judgment. Physical works follow a waterfall lifecycle. The digital platform follows a spiral lifecycle (Boehm, 1988) with agile delivery inside each spiral (Project Management Institute & Agile Alliance, 2017).

Why this valley, and why a pandemic program

The Fraser Valley is both a place where people must eat and a place that feeds a much larger province. Dairy, poultry, eggs, berries, and field vegetables are concentrated on a floodplain that has already shown how quickly it can be cut off. On November 14, 2021, a strong atmospheric river brought extreme precipitation to southwestern British Columbia. The resulting floods and landslides isolated communities by road and rail and became, on the account of Gillett et al. (2022), the costliest natural disaster in the province’s history. A pandemic that arrives with a similar interruption to Highway 1, the Sumas corridor, or cross-border trucking would combine a labour shock with a geography shock.

Public-health guidance already expects jurisdictions to plan for influenza pandemics as social and operational events, not only as clinical ones (Public Health Agency of Canada, 2018; World Health Organization, 2017). British Columbia’s emergency statute likewise expects coordinated action across governments (Emergency and Disaster Management Act, 2023). FoodShield sits in that gap: the logistics of food for people who cannot use ordinary retail during a wave, designed before the wave, and exercised before it is needed.

The investment case is not a claim that one cheque produces a private profit. It is a public option. Hubs, vehicles, and a logistics platform are useful only if they still work when staff are ill, the grid is strained, and trust is thin. That is why backup power, a shelf-stable core that does not depend on refrigeration, offline operating procedures, and published allocation rules are treated as scope, not as enhancements. The payback discussion shows the arithmetic openly, including the finding that household food replacement alone does not recover a billion dollars.

Three core deliverables

Everything else in the work breakdown structure supports these three products. Acceptance conditions are defined on the scope page, timed on the schedule, and funded on the cost page.

Deliverable 1

Resilience hubs

Five primary hubs, at Abbotsford, Chilliwack, Mission, Hope, and Kent–Agassiz, with cold rooms, dry storage, backup power, potable water, and flood and seismic hardening. Together they hold a 90-day shelf-stable reserve for a priority population and a shorter chilled buffer. Hope is protected scope because a flood can split the valley.

Hub acceptance criteria

Deliverable 2

Emergency distribution fleet

Refrigerated heavy units, medium trucks, and last-mile electric vans, plus two depots with charging and maintenance. The service target is delivery to designated community points in every listed municipality within 24 hours of activation. A duty-cycle study decides how much of the eastern run can be electric in winter; the contract will not assume a brochure range.

Fleet procurement

Deliverable 3

Digital logistics platform

A system that records inventory, allocates stock against an agreed priority rule, and still functions when the network does not. Personal information, if any, stays in Canada and is assessed under public-sector privacy law. The platform is built in spirals and is not allowed to be the only record of food temperature or stock.

Processing plant cluster outbreak

Trigger event

A respiratory cluster closes two further-processing lines in Abbotsford. Absenteeism and a precautionary lot hold cut chilled protein into local retail within the same shift. Public Health Agency of Canada, 2018

Immediate protocol response (0–6 hours)

The Abbotsford hub moves from standby to partial activation. The platform freezes outbound lots from the affected plants, points allocation at the shelf-stable core plus unaffected chilled stock, and calls the paid surge roster. Cooler alarms stay on the path that does not depend on the platform.

Secondary stabilization actions (6–48 hours)

Refrigerated vans run priority parcels to community points in Abbotsford, Mission, and Chilliwack. Hope’s pre-positioned stock is not drawn west. Short-dated protein from the held lots is kept out of rotation. Fraser Health’s liaison confirms the list is based on need, not on who arrives first.

Operational deliverable involved

Resilience hubs, led by the Abbotsford hub, with the digital logistics platform holding the lot freeze and the allocation rule.

Metrics monitored

  • Cold-storage utilization, Abbotsford: 71%, divert to Chilliwack if it reaches 85%.
  • Quarantined chilled lots: 14, excluded from the countable reserve.
  • Emergency buffer release: 18,000 person-days in the first six hours.
  • Surge roster reporting: 82% of called positions filled.

Where the argument is developed

The overview is the map. The graded argument lives on the seven pages below. Each page can be read on its own, and each one uses the same ceiling, the same date, and the same disclaimer.

  • Scope, charter, and deliverables Charter, champion and authority, work breakdown structure, inclusions and exclusions, and a full SWOT.
  • Time and schedule Waterfall and spiral lifecycles, the five process groups, a quarter-by-quarter Gantt from 2027 to 2029, and the critical path into December 31, 2029.
  • Cost, budget, and procurement Line-by-line allocation of CAD $1,000,000,000, vendor strategy, cost of quality, and an illustrated payback that shows its assumptions.
  • People, stakeholders, and organization Four frames of organization, three spheres of systems management, rightsholder and stakeholder registers, staffing, and motivation.
  • Risk management and quality Top 10 pandemic risks with severity, likelihood, and controls; the quality plan; test stages; and the reporting cadence.
  • Closing and handover The transition that places operational ownership with municipal and health authorities on or before December 31, 2029.
  • Academic references APA 7th edition sources for food security, public health, emergency law, and project management standards.
